# YouTube Course: index

Docs for Mohammad's AI teaching channel, which runs from beginner level to current research. Written by Claude, 17 November 2026.

## Docs

The live docs' text uses the new course numbers (updated 19 November 2026). Their titles, used as the link texts below, still show the original numbers until they are renamed on claude.ai; the mapping is under Status.

- [Four Courses on Current AI Research](https://claude.ai/artifact/N1vPB4dx5dB7hgC71M4f61): syllabi for four 12-session courses (geometric deep learning, privacy-preserving ML, LLMs, and mathematics and AI), the session format, capstones, instructor notes and sources.

- [Course 4 Content: Mathematics and AI](https://claude.ai/artifact/6P1AhAgPTfN7C5oHEyrY39): ready-to-teach content for all 12 sessions of Course 9.
- [Course 5: Winning Online AI Competitions](https://claude.ai/artifact/1Q1dA1MEUvrTKno5QDYVYn): its own syllabus (with readings, labs and a live-competition capstone) plus ready-to-teach content for all 12 sessions, from choosing a contest and validation through tabular, vision, LLM and reasoning competitions to ensembling and write-ups.
- [Course 6: From Beginner to an AI Research Lab](https://claude.ai/artifact/NLVeGztHw1T3K2WzqAxodX): its own syllabus (with readings, labs and a reproduce-and-extend capstone) plus ready-to-teach content for all 12 sessions, from lab types and foundations (math, code, ML, deep learning, transformers) through reading, reproducing, experiments, project scoping and writing to applications, cold emails and interviews. Every session also has talking pointers for the camera, a step-by-step lab guide (starter code, expected results, common problems) and a graded 10-point assignment. The doc also has a "Delivering the sessions on camera" section, a staged 12–18 month roadmap with gates, a readiness check, and a toolkit: catch-up and stretch tasks per session, a capstone paper shortlist, rubrics, templates, fallback plans and an interview question bank.
- [Course 7: Building a Small Bangla Language Model](https://claude.ai/artifact/EeFta9sLimwtx9t4D9aDRn): syllabus plus all 12 sessions, each with talking pointers, three segments, a lab guide, a quiz, a misconception and a graded assignment. Students take a 5–30M-parameter Bangla GPT from raw text to a public release: Unicode cleaning, corpus, tokenizer, model, training on free GPUs, evaluation, instruction tuning against a LoRA baseline, compression, responsible release.
- [Course 8: Research Engineering Interviews](https://claude.ai/artifact/XiiapdFjeF7Mc3tLeDqyna): syllabus plus all 12 sessions in the same full format. Covers the interview loop, coding, tensors, ML from scratch, debugging, fundamentals, estimates, system design, research discussion, behavioral rounds and take-homes, ending in a four-round mock loop.
- Course 3: Ten Years of Deep Learning Ideas (`Course_3_Ten_Years_of_Deep_Learning_Ideas/course_document.md` in this repository, rendered to the folder's PDF): syllabus, capstone with rubric, a table of where the decade's other ideas are taught, and all 12 sessions in the full format (talking pointers, three segments, lab guide, quiz, misconception, graded assignment, sources). Covers residuals, normalization and µP; optimizers and training at scale; the generalization puzzles; vision transformers; self-supervised learning; in-context learning; VAEs, flows and GANs; diffusion; flow matching; deep RL; neural fields; robustness, uncertainty and calibration.
- Course 14: AI Research in 2026 (`Course_14_AI_Research_in_2026/course_document.md`, rendered to the folder's PDF): the same format for research published or presented from January to mid-November 2026: reading the year's research; agent time horizons; RL for reasoning; multi-turn reliability; diffusion language models; transformer expressivity; Polar Express and Muon; provable grokking; high-accuracy sampling; probes and obfuscation; AI and the Erdős problems; feed-forward 4D reconstruction.
- Course 2: Mathematics for Machine Learning (`Course_2_Mathematics_for_Machine_Learning/course_document.md`, rendered to the folder's PDF): the same format for the mathematics machine learning uses, each idea worked by hand and then checked in NumPy: vectors and matrices; linear systems, rank and least squares; eigenvalues, SVD and PCA; gradients, Jacobians and the chain rule; automatic differentiation; probability; estimation (MLE, MAP and confidence intervals); gradient descent and convexity; SGD, Newton's method and constraints; numerical methods; information theory; graphs from PageRank to graph neural networks. It was added after the renumbering; take it with or after Course 1.

- Course 10: Deep Learning on Curved Spaces (`Course_10_Deep_Learning_on_Curved_Spaces/course_document.md`, rendered to the folder's PDF): Riemannian geometry for machine learning in the same full format, built from the sphere upward and checked in code: why curved spaces; manifolds, tangent spaces and metrics; geodesics, exponential maps and the Fréchet mean; curvature and hyperbolic space; Riemannian optimization; the Poincaré and Lorentz models; hyperbolic embeddings; hyperbolic neural networks; SPD matrices; SO(3) and SE(3); generative models on manifolds; mixed curvature.
- Course 11: On-Device AI (`Course_11_On_Device_AI/course_document.md`): fitting models onto phones and microcontrollers, every technique measured: cost and the roofline; efficient architectures; quantization (two sessions); pruning; distillation; ONNX and runtimes; memory and the KV cache; small language models and speech on phones; TinyML; benchmarking; learning on the device.
- Course 12: AI for Accessibility (`Course_12_AI_for_Accessibility/course_document.md`): AI that helps disabled people hear, see, read and communicate, and how it fails: disability and accessibility standards; Bangla speech recognition; captions; sign languages and hand pose; isolated signs; continuous signing and translation; lip reading; image description; reading support; AAC; evaluation, fairness and participatory design; deployment in Bangladesh.
- Course 13: Machine Learning for Agriculture and Climate (`Course_13_ML_for_Agriculture_and_Climate/course_document.md`): Bangladesh as the running example: problems and data; plant disease; remote sensing; crop mapping; yield; forecast skill; machine-learning weather prediction; floods; downscaling and bias correction; extremes and decisions; farm sensors; responsible deployment.
- Course 16: Algorithms Behind Modern AI (`Course_16_Algorithms_Behind_Modern_AI/course_document.md`): the data structures and algorithms inside AI systems, for students who know data structures: counting cost and memory layout; hashing, the hashing trick and Bloom filters; tokenizers with heaps, linked lists and tries; computation graphs and topological sort; edit distance and alignment; Viterbi, the forward algorithm and CTC; beam search and sampling; automata and constrained decoding; k-d trees, LSH and MinHash; HNSW, IVF and product quantization; caches, queues and batching; randomized algorithms for data pipelines.

Each content doc gives every session three video segments with pause-and-try questions, a lab, a three-question quiz and one misconception to address, with answers and a sources list.

## Notebooks

- All eight courses now have one Jupyter notebook per session (96 in all). They were delivered to Mohammad in chat on 17 November 2026 as one bundle, `AI_YouTube_Courses.zip`, which also holds a PDF export of every course document and a README. The notebooks are not stored in this project.
- Courses 2, 3 and 10–14 (84 notebooks, written 17–19 November 2026) are stored in this repository and saved with their outputs. They were executed with `tools/run_notebook.py`, which runs every cell except NOT RUN cells, and then re-run independently; every printed number reproduced, apart from timings. Each finishes in about two minutes or less on a CPU.
- Course 3 labs: deep networks with and without residuals and normalization, a µP coordinate check and learning-rate transfer, AdamW, Lion and Muon from scratch, double descent and the edge of stability, a tiny ViT against a CNN, SimCLR/SimSiam/MAE, in-context linear regression, VAE/VQ-VAE/RealNVP/GAN, DDPM and DDIM, flow matching and reflow, PPO on a hand-written CartPole and MCTS, Fourier features/SIREN and a toy radiance field, adversarial attacks, calibration and conformal prediction.
- Course 14 labs are toy illustrations, not reproductions: benchmark noise and paired tests, time-horizon fits, GRPO entropy and clipping, a sharded-conversation simulator, decoding order in a masked diffusion model, automata blow-up and an attention recognizer, polar-factor iterations inside Muon, grokking in ridge regression, ULA against MALA, training against a probe, Kummer's theorem and unit-distance counting, per-scene against feed-forward reconstruction.
- Course 2 labs: nearest neighbours with loops and with one matrix product, least squares three ways and what a duplicated feature does, power iteration, Eckart–Young and PCA on digits, gradient checks, forward and reverse mode with a hand-written scalar engine, sampling and Bayes' rule, MLE and MAP with bootstrap intervals, gradient descent and conditioning, SGD noise, Newton and L-BFGS, Lagrange multipliers, float16 and bfloat16 failures, stable softmax, conjugate gradients, forward and reverse KL fits, perplexity, PageRank, spectral clustering, and one GCN layer with oversmoothing.
- Course 10 labs: averaging angles, rotations and covariance matrices; tangent projections and metrics; exp and log maps, Karcher means and holonomy on the sphere; Sarkar's tree embedding and float precision; Riemannian gradient descent on the sphere and Stiefel manifold, and Procrustes alignment; Möbius operations and model conversions; Poincaré embeddings of a tree; δ-hyperbolicity and a hyperbolic MLP; SPD classifiers for synthetic EEG; rotation regression with three representations; Riemannian flow matching on the sphere; product-space embeddings.
- Course 11 labs: parameter, byte and MAC counts and a measured roofline; depthwise-separable CNNs; quantization from scratch with integer-only inference; QAT and group-wise LLM-style quantization; magnitude and channel pruning; distillation; ONNX export, ONNX Runtime and int8; peak memory and KV-cache sizing; decoding speed of a tiny language model; a keyword spotter within microcontroller budgets; a benchmark harness; on-device personalization and federated averaging. Sizes and accuracies reproduce; latencies vary between runs.
- Course 12 labs (synthetic data throughout): WCAG contrast and colour-vision simulation; log-mel features and a CTC recognizer; DTW, forced alignment and caption segmentation; normalized hand-pose classification; GRU and ST-GCN sign recognition on new signers; CTC over glosses and BLEU; a graph-attention lip reader with audio-visual fusion; photo-quality checks and abstention; a CTC line recognizer and text normalization; word prediction and switch scanning; per-group evaluation with bootstrap intervals and SUS; quantizing a sign model.
- Course 13 labs (synthetic data standing in for the real sources): leakage across districts and years; lab-to-field shift in leaf images; NDVI, cloud masks and compositing; crop mapping with spatial cross-validation; yield models in unseen years; forecast skill, CRPS and Brier scores; a learned Lorenz-96 model; radar flood mapping and a rainfall–runoff LSTM; quantile mapping and CNN downscaling; GEV return levels and the cost–loss model; Kalman filtering of farm sensors; carbon estimates, Bangla advice text and a model card.
- Course 16 labs (pure Python and NumPy, synthetic data, exact operation counts next to timings): attention's multiply–adds, strides and CSR; a hash table, the hashing trick, a Bloom filter and a count–min sketch; a naive and an indexed BPE trainer and two encoders; topological sort, a backward sweep checked against PyTorch, and graph optimizations; edit distance, WER, ROUGE-L and Needleman–Wunsch; Viterbi segmentation, an HMM and a CTC loss checked against PyTorch; greedy, beam and exact search, top-k, top-p and the Gumbel trick; a trie, a small regular-expression compiler and token masking; a k-d tree, random-hyperplane LSH, MinHash and near-duplicate clusters; a navigable small-world graph, HNSW, IVF and product quantization; KV-cache counts, an LRU cache, a prefix tree and a batching simulator; reservoir sampling, shuffles, the alias method and a sum tree.
- Every code cell that is not labelled NOT RUN (or left as a student TODO stub) was executed once on CPU, and every notebook passes nbformat validation. Runnable cells use small built-in or synthetic data, so their numbers are illustrations, not benchmark results.
- Cells labelled NOT RUN need a GPU, a download, an account or a library that was not installed (PyTorch Geometric, escnn, e3nn, OGB, Boltz-2, Opacus, Flower, TenSEAL, CatBoost, cuDF, TabPFN, timm, transformers, peft, bitsandbytes, trl, vLLM, LeanDojo, nanoGPT, MNIST). They were written from documented interfaces and have not been executed.
- Course 7: hand-written GCN, GIN, WL test, positional encodings, C4-equivariant CNN, EGNN, Poincaré embeddings, a small graph diffusion model, graph retrieval, spatio-temporal forecasting and link prediction, all on synthetic or scikit-learn data.
- Course 8: hand-written DP-SGD with a Rényi accountant (checked against the value in Abadi et al. 2016), canary auditing, FedAvg and FedProx, gradient inversion, DP-LoRA on a tiny GPT, a toy Paillier scheme (teaching only), private graph aggregation, SISA and PATE.
- Course 4: a toy Bangla grammar language with a tiny GPT, used for scaling laws, quantization, KV cache, speculative decoding, QLoRA, DPO, masked diffusion, a sparse autoencoder and distillation; GRPO on arithmetic; a scripted stand-in model for the agent harness.
- Course 9: groups, characters, steerable kernel bases, grokking (p = 47, small MLP), categories of layers, lenses, weight sharing from symmetry, sheaf diffusion, pass@k, counterexample search for wrong formalizations, and an evolve-and-evaluate cap-set search. The Lean 4 snippets in sessions 9 and 11 were NOT compiled.
- Course 6: synthetic competitions for metrics, leaderboard shake-up, validation, leaks, target encoding (LightGBM and XGBoost were run), forecasting, TTA, preference prediction, time budgets, blending and reproduction.
- Course 1: the lab code from the course doc with expected values asserted (sessions 2–6 and 9), and worksheets with checking helpers for the other sessions.
- Course 5 notebooks run on a 16-sentence Bangla sample. Course 15 notebooks give stubs with tests and reference solutions at the bottom.
- Results that came out against expectation are stated in the notebooks: plain DPO breaks the toy model without an anchoring loss (C4 s6); fine-tuning did not generalize to held-out objects (C4 s5); the position-embedding ablation showed no measurable difference (C1 s6); random search matched the evolutionary loop on cap sets (C9 s12); equal-weight blends were worse than the best single model (C6 s10); cosine similarity did not beat Euclidean distance on digits (C2 s1); the normal equations returned meaningless weights without warning for most duplicated features (C2 s2).
