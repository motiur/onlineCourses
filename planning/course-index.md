Docs for Mohammad's AI teaching channel, which runs from beginner level to current research. Written by Claude, 17 November 2026.

The live docs keep their original titles, which use the old course numbers; the mapping to the new numbers is under Status.

- [Course 6: From Beginner to an AI Research Lab](https://claude.ai/artifact/NLVeGztHw1T3K2WzqAxodX): its own syllabus (with readings, labs and a reproduce-and-extend capstone) plus ready-to-teach content for all 12 sessions, from lab types and foundations (math, code, ML, deep learning, transformers) through reading, reproducing, experiments, project scoping and writing to applications, cold emails and interviews. Every session also has talking pointers for the camera, a step-by-step lab guide (starter code, expected results, common problems) and a graded 10-point assignment. The doc also has a "Delivering the sessions on camera" section, a staged 12–18 month roadmap with gates, a readiness check, and a toolkit: catch-up and stretch tasks per session, a capstone paper shortlist, rubrics, templates, fallback plans and an interview question bank.
- [Course 7: Building a Small Bangla Language Model](https://claude.ai/artifact/EeFta9sLimwtx9t4D9aDRn): syllabus plus all 12 sessions, each with talking pointers, three segments, a lab guide, a quiz, a misconception and a graded assignment. Students take a 5–30M-parameter Bangla GPT from raw text to a public release: Unicode cleaning, corpus, tokenizer, model, training on free GPUs, evaluation, instruction tuning against a LoRA baseline, compression, responsible release.
- [Course 8: Research Engineering Interviews](https://claude.ai/artifact/XiiapdFjeF7Mc3tLeDqyna): syllabus plus all 12 sessions in the same full format. Covers the interview loop, coding, tensors, ML from scratch, debugging, fundamentals, estimates, system design, research discussion, behavioral rounds and take-homes, ending in a four-round mock loop.
- Course 2: Ten Years of Deep Learning Ideas (`Course_2_Ten_Years_of_Deep_Learning_Ideas/course_document.md` in this repository, rendered to the folder's PDF): syllabus, capstone with rubric, a table of where the decade's other ideas are taught, and all 12 sessions in the full format (talking pointers, three segments, lab guide, quiz, misconception, graded assignment, sources). Covers residuals, normalization and µP; optimizers and training at scale; the generalization puzzles; vision transformers; self-supervised learning; in-context learning; VAEs, flows and GANs; diffusion; flow matching; deep RL; neural fields; robustness, uncertainty and calibration.
- Course 9: AI Research in 2026 (`Course_9_AI_Research_in_2026/course_document.md`, rendered to the folder's PDF): the same format for research published or presented from January to mid-November 2026: reading the year's research; agent time horizons; RL for reasoning; multi-turn reliability; diffusion language models; transformer expressivity; Polar Express and Muon; provable grokking; high-accuracy sampling; probes and obfuscation; AI and the Erdős problems; feed-forward 4D reconstruction.

Each content doc gives every session three video segments with pause-and-try questions, a lab, a three-question quiz and one misconception to address, with answers and a sources list.

## Notebooks

- All eight courses now have one Jupyter notebook per session (96 in all). They were delivered to Mohammad in chat on 17 November 2026 as one bundle, `AI_YouTube_Courses.zip`, which also holds a PDF export of every course document and a README. The notebooks are not stored in this project.
- Courses 2 and 9 (24 notebooks, written 17–18 November 2026) are stored in this repository and saved with their outputs. They were executed with `tools/run_notebook.py`, which runs every cell except NOT RUN cells, and then re-run independently; every printed number reproduced, apart from timings. Each finishes in about two minutes or less on a CPU.
- Course 2 labs: deep networks with and without residuals and normalization, a µP coordinate check and learning-rate transfer, AdamW, Lion and Muon from scratch, double descent and the edge of stability, a tiny ViT against a CNN, SimCLR/SimSiam/MAE, in-context linear regression, VAE/VQ-VAE/RealNVP/GAN, DDPM and DDIM, flow matching and reflow, PPO on a hand-written CartPole and MCTS, Fourier features/SIREN and a toy radiance field, adversarial attacks, calibration and conformal prediction.
- Course 9 labs are toy illustrations, not reproductions: benchmark noise and paired tests, time-horizon fits, GRPO entropy and clipping, a sharded-conversation simulator, decoding order in a masked diffusion model, automata blow-up and an attention recognizer, polar-factor iterations inside Muon, grokking in ridge regression, ULA against MALA, training against a probe, Kummer's theorem and unit-distance counting, per-scene against feed-forward reconstruction.
- Every code cell that is not labelled NOT RUN (or left as a student TODO stub) was executed once on CPU, and every notebook passes nbformat validation. Runnable cells use small built-in or synthetic data, so their numbers are illustrations, not benchmark results.
- Cells labelled NOT RUN need a GPU, a download, an account or a library that was not installed (PyTorch Geometric, escnn, e3nn, OGB, Boltz-2, Opacus, Flower, TenSEAL, CatBoost, cuDF, TabPFN, timm, transformers, peft, bitsandbytes, trl, vLLM, LeanDojo, nanoGPT, MNIST). They were written from documented interfaces and have not been executed.
- Course 6: hand-written GCN, GIN, WL test, positional encodings, C4-equivariant CNN, EGNN, Poincaré embeddings, a small graph diffusion model, graph retrieval, spatio-temporal forecasting and link prediction, all on synthetic or scikit-learn data.
- Course 7: hand-written DP-SGD with a Rényi accountant (checked against the value in Abadi et al. 2016), canary auditing, FedAvg and FedProx, gradient inversion, DP-LoRA on a tiny GPT, a toy Paillier scheme (teaching only), private graph aggregation, SISA and PATE.
- Course 3: a toy Bangla grammar language with a tiny GPT, used for scaling laws, quantization, KV cache, speculative decoding, QLoRA, DPO, masked diffusion, a sparse autoencoder and distillation; GRPO on arithmetic; a scripted stand-in model for the agent harness.
- Course 8: groups, characters, steerable kernel bases, grokking (p = 47, small MLP), categories of layers, lenses, weight sharing from symmetry, sheaf diffusion, pass@k, counterexample search for wrong formalizations, and an evolve-and-evaluate cap-set search. The Lean 4 snippets in sessions 9 and 11 were NOT compiled.
- Course 5: synthetic competitions for metrics, leaderboard shake-up, validation, leaks, target encoding (LightGBM and XGBoost were run), forecasting, TTA, preference prediction, time budgets, blending and reproduction.
- Course 1: the lab code from the course doc with expected values asserted (sessions 2–6 and 9), and worksheets with checking helpers for the other sessions.
- Course 4 notebooks run on a 16-sentence Bangla sample. Course 10 notebooks give stubs with tests and reference solutions at the bottom.
- Results that came out against expectation are stated in the notebooks: plain DPO breaks the toy model without an anchoring loss (C3 s6); fine-tuning did not generalize to held-out objects (C3 s5); the position-embedding ablation showed no measurable difference (C1 s6); random search matched the evolutionary loop on cap sets (C8 s12); equal-weight blends were worse than the best single model (C5 s10).
